Translated from Romanien to English
Argeș Monastery (the Craftsman Manole)
collected by Vasile Alecsandri

Down by the River Argeș,
On one beautiful shore,
Voivode Black (Negru Vodă) is passing,
Along with ten other companions.
Nine great masters,
Apprentices and Masons,
With Manole, the tenth,
Who is also the best.
They all go along the way
To choose a valley,
Grounds for a Monastery,
And for remembrance.
Look, as they went
Down the road they met
A pitiful shepherd,
Who was playing a sorrowful tune on his flute.
As soon as he saw him,
The Voivode said towards him:
“Proud shepherd who
Sings a sad tune on your flute!
Up on the River Argeș,
With your flock you roamed.
Down the River Argeș
With your flocks you went.
Haven't you seen
Wherever you've been
A lonesome brick wall
That was left unfinished,
On an uphill place,
Surrounded by green hazel trees?”
“Indeed, Voivode, I've seen
Where I've been
A lonesome brick wall
That was left unfinished.
As soon as dogs see it,
They charge towards it
And they bark of desolated land
And they howl of death.”
Upon hearing this
The Voivode was in bliss,
Quickly he departed,
To reach the brick wall,
Together with nine masons,
Nine great masters,
And Manole was the tenth,
Who is also the best.
“Here it is, my wall!
Here is where I choose,
Grounds for a Monastery
And for remembrance.
Thus, you great masters,
Apprentices and Masons,
Soon you must beseech
The work to begin,
So you can raise for me,
Here to enforce for me
A tall Monastery
As no other has ever been
. I shall give you fortunes
, I will make you noblemen.
If you do not, afterwards
I shall place you in the walls,
I will wall you alive
And set you as foundation.”
The masters were rushing,
Tethering the ropes,
Measuring the spot,
Great ditches they dug,
And they worked tirelessly,
The wall to build up,
Yet all they had built,
It fell during the night!
The second day, again,
The third day, again,
The fourth day, again
They were working in vain!
The Voivode was astonished
And then he rebuked them
And then he frowned
And threatened them
He shall bury all alive
Right in the foundations!
The great masters,
Apprentices and Masons,
Were trembling and working,
Were working and trembling
On a long hot summer's day
All day till dusk;
Yet, Manole was sitting,
He was not even working,
He instead went sleeping,
And a dream he was dreaming.
Then he was waking,
And thus he was speaking:
"Nine great masters,
Apprentices and Masons!
Do you know what I dreamt
When I went to bed?
A whisper from above
Spoke truth to me,
Said all that we work on
Will be ruined by sunrise,
Until we decide
To place in the walls The first of our wives,
The first of our sisters
Which will arrive
Tomorrow, early in the morning
Bearing food
For her husband or brother.
If you wish
The work to be done
For the monastery
For remembrance
We shall begin
For all to keep the mystery;
For any wife,
For any sister
We shall sacrifice her at noon
In the walls we shall wall her up!"
The morning has come,
Manea (Manole) woke up,
And then up he climbed,
On a fence made of twigs,
And higher still on the scaffolds.
He peered towards the fields,
Scouting the road.
When, alas! What did he see?
Who was coming near?
It was his own wife,
Flower of the fields.
She was drawing near
And was bearing
Food for his lunch,
Wine for his thirst.
As soon as he saw her
His heart skipped a beat,
He fell to his knees
And said through his tears:
“Please, God, send here
A furious storm,
Springs to appear,
So they could flow,
The rivers to grow,
My beloved to stop,
To stop her in the valley,
To return her on her way!"
God showed mercy,
He listened to his plea,
He gathered the clouds,
He darkened the skies,
And suddenly starts to pour
Unforgiving rains,
Which were forming springs,
And rivers overflowed.
But as much as it rained,
The lover couldn’t be stopped,
She kept walking along,
She kept drawing near.
Manole saw her,
His heart was weeping,
And again made the sign of the cross
And again he prayed:
"Blow, God, a tempest,
Blow it over Earth,
Fir trees to peel,
Maple trees to bend,
Mountains to collapse,
So that my lover goes away,
And she turns away
And goes to the valley!"
God showed mercy,
He listened to his plea,
A tempest He blew
A tempest over the Earth,
Maple trees it bent
Fir trees it peeled.
Mountains it collapsed,
But Ana
Away didn't turn!
She kept coming
Along the way she stumbled
And slowly she neared,
Woe was on her,
Here she had arrived!
The great masters,
Apprentices and masons
Cheered up,
As they saw her.
Yet, Manea was maddened,
His lover he kissed,
And embraced her
Up on the scaffolds he took her,
Near the wall he placed her,
And jokingly said to her:
"Stay put, my beloved,
Don't be afraid,
As a joke we would like
To wall you alive!"
Ana trusted him
And cheerfully laughed.
Yet Manea sighed
And he began
The wall to build,
The dream to make true.
The wall kept going up
And encompassed her,
Up to her ankles,
Up to her thighs,
And her, woe be on her!
She didn't laugh anymore
And kept saying:
"Manole, Manole,
Craftsman Manole!
Enough with this joke
It's not funny anymore, dear!
Manole, Manole,
Mason Manole,
The wall grips me tight
It breaks my frail body!"
Yet Manea kept silent
And kept laying the wall,
The wall kept going up
And encompassed her,
Up to her ankles,
Up to her thighs,
Up to her ribs,
Up to her breasts.
The woe was on her,
She always cried
And kept pleading:
"Manole, Manole,
Mason Manole!
The wall grips me tight,
It crushes my breasts,
My newborn is being crushed!"
Manole was maddened,
Kept laying the wall,
And the wall kept going up
And encompassed her,
Up to her ribs,
Up to her breasts,
Up to her lips,
Up to her eyes,
Such that she, woe be on her,
Couldn't be seen,
But could be heard,
From the wall she talked:
"Manoli, Manoli,
Mason Manoli!
The bad wall grips me tight,
Takes away my life's light!"
Down by the River Argeș,
On one beautiful shore,
Voivode Black comes,
So he can praise
That monastery,
So masterfully built,
A tall Monastery,
As none has ever been.
The lord gazed upon it
And became cheerful,
And this he said:
"You all, master craftsman,
Ten master masons,
Tell me and be honest,
Vow with the hand on your chest,
If you're skilled enough
For me to build
Another monastery,
For remembrance,
Much more filled with light
And with much more beauty?"
And those great masters,
Apprentices and masons,
As they sat on the steeple,
And up on the roof,
Cheerfully they prided,
And thus they answered:
"As us, great masters,
Apprentices and masons,
None others exist
On this Earth!
Know that we are able
To build at any time
Any other monastery,
For remembrance,
Much more filled with light
And with much more beauty!"
The lord listened to them
And contemplated,
Then he gave order,
The scaffolds to destroy,
The ladders to be taken away,
And those craftsman,
Ten great masons,
To be abandoned,
To be left to rot,
Up on the steeple,
Up on the roof.
The masters were thinking,
And started tinkering,
Wings for them to fly,
Made from light shingles.
Then they spread them open,
In the sky they jumped,
But fell to the ground.
Where they were crashing,
Their bodies had smashed.
And poor Manoli,
Mason Manoli,
As soon as he tried,
As soon as he jumped,
He heard
Coming from the wall,
A suppressed call
A voice he much loved,
That kept lamenting
And kept saying:
"Manole, Manole,
Mason Manole!
The wall grips me tight
It crushes my breasts,
My newborn is being crushed!
It takes away my life's light!"
As he heard her,
Manea lost himself,
His eyes got veiled,
His world went upside down,
The clouds kept spinning,
And up from the steeple,
Up from the roof,
To his death he fell!
And there where he fell
What else occurred?
A calm fountain,
Holding little water,
The water was salty,
From the tears that wet it!
